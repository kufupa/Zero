# Pre-Flight Check — AI Insurance Shopping

**Phase:** 0.5 — Pre-Flight
**Project:** insurance-shopping-agent
**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Confidence:** High for incumbent presence and consumer shopping; Medium for unmet workflow gap; Low for the unconfirmed startup thesis.

---

## Findings

- ⚠️ **Dominant/existing solutions:** The category is not open. Insurify, Compare.com, The Zebra, Jerry, Nsure, Experian/Gabi, Policygenius and others provide free comparison, quotes and varying degrees of purchase/agent support. Insurify says it serves 120+ carrier partners and 500M+ integrations [company-reported; current partner counts not independently audited]. More directly, Insurify's full real-time auto-quote ChatGPT experience is live in 11 states as of August 2026, with a 40+ state expansion planned [company-reported]. Foresee already provides estimates and browser-agent carrier quote confirmation. Sources: [Insurify ChatGPT rollout](https://insurify.com/press/news/insurify-chatgpt-plugin-upgrade/) · [Foresee docs](https://go-foresee.com/docs)
- ✅ **Demand exists:** J.D. Power's June 4, 2026 U.S. Insurance Shopping Study reports that 53% of auto customers shopped in the prior year, shoppers averaged 3.5 quotes, and 48% of new auto policies were bought digitally. This supports shopping demand—not demand for this particular product. [J.D. Power](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2026-us-insurance-shopping-study/)
- ⚠️ **Monetization exists, but is competitive and conditional:** Free-to-consumer is conventional. Carriers/agency partners pay commissions when a policy is bound, or providers buy qualified leads/clicks/calls. Large incumbents report hundreds of millions in revenue, but those figures do not disclose a startup's CAC, quote-to-bind rate or payout. Specific commission/lead rates are not public in reviewed evidence.
- ⚠️ **Regulatory gate:** Personalized insurance recommendations, discussing terms, soliciting, application completion and sale can trigger producer licensing, appointment, compensation, advertising and supervision rules; state law controls. Referral fees may be restricted or cannot be purchase-contingent in some states. This is a legal workstream, not a simple “partner has a license” workaround. Sources: [NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/model-law-218.pdf) · [Washington OIC advisory](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-09/taa-2021-01.pdf) · [NY DFS referral opinion](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2008/rg080425.htm)
- ⚠️ **Possible wedge, not yet proven:** Review snippets and public terms surface repeated information entry, redirects/hand-offs, unclear final price, unwanted contact concerns, and failed payment/binding episodes. These are purposively sampled anecdotes; they do not prove prevalence or an unserved market. More promising thesis may be quote-to-bind continuity, comparison quality, permissioned data reuse, an embedded channel, or agency workflow—not merely AI chat.

## Initial verdict
**Yellow light — investigate, do not pitch generic AI comparison yet.** The market and monetization pools are real, but product scope and founder advantage are unknown, incumbent pressure is high, and regulated execution plus carrier access create material barriers. Proceed to founder intake and focused research; do not produce a confident forecast yet.

## Red Flags
- Insurify already occupies the obvious “real quotes inside AI chat” territory in multiple states and is expanding.
- No distinctive capability, distribution advantage, team-market fit, or customer evidence has been provided.
- Licensing and carrier access could invalidate a seemingly easy lead/commission model.

## Yellow Flags
- Market rates are cyclical; carrier acquisition budgets can change with underwriting profitability.
- The customer-voice sample is anecdotal; home insurance evidence is especially sparse.
- Insurify and Foresee company claims on quote breadth, outcomes, AI traffic, and partner access are not equivalent to independent validation.
