# Founder Intake — Provisional Brief

**Phase:** 1 — Intake
**Project:** insurance-shopping-agent
**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Confidence:** Low; founder concept and team information are still unconfirmed.

---

## What is known

- **Goal:** Research an ambitious, potentially billion-dollar insurance startup idea and whether an evidence-backed version could make a credible YC case; do not assume Foresee's current product or Europe-first LOIs define the final thesis.
- **Starting reference:** Foresee (`go-foresee.com`), a consumer AI insurance estimate/quote-confirmation experience. The founder/team relationship to Foresee, present product ownership and execution status are not yet documented.
- **Market ambition:** Prioritize U.S. scale; do not overfit strategy to existing European LOIs.
- **Founder-reported commercial access:** One LOI from French commercial brokerage SATEC and one LOI from an MGA specializing in clinical-trial/biopharma insurance. Terms, signatories, budget, payment, workflow/data access, procurement and conversion commitments have not been reviewed or independently verified.
- **Founder preference:** Run startup-design, startup-competitors, and startup-positioning playbooks; find a direction through research rather than requiring the founder to choose a model before that work. The founder says the existing European LOIs should not dominate the ambition/market choice. Formal customer interviews were initially deferred; none are documented in this project.
- **YC goal:** Extreme ambition, not a claim of YC readiness. The founder wants to test a path to $1M ARR as quickly as evidence allows and described a “$10 million unicorn status” goal; whether $10M means ARR or another milestone is unclear. These are targets to assess, not forecast assumptions.
- **Team context:** The founder offered to share team details, but bios, roles, strengths and credentials are not yet documented.

## Not yet known (do not infer)

- Exact product and job-to-be-done, who pays, product scope, first state/line/segment/channel and regulatory structure. These are to be inferred through the broad research and validated with buyers, not demanded from founder upfront.
- Founder/cofounder experience, roles, insurance licenses, technical capability, domain insight, U.S. distribution and unfair advantage.
- Foresee's ownership/relationship to the founders, whether it is live or prototype, actual quote/confirmation/bind behavior, users, usage, revenue, partners and current execution.
- LOI contents beyond founder-reported counterparties and broad domains; signatories, commercial budget, payment, workflow/data access, procurement, scope and conversion terms.
- Existing customer conversations, traction, budget/runway, time commitment and hiring constraints.
- Actual pricing, CAC, customer acquisition rate, sales cycle, gross margin, persistency and renewal revenue.

## Provisional research hypothesis — not approved product direction

A U.S. consumer shopping flow for auto insurance, assisted by AI and potentially distributed in an AI assistant. Home/bundling remains adjacent, not assumed MVP scope. Research tests it against agency workflow SaaS/API and embedded distribution alternatives. Quote-to-bind authority remains undecided.

## Intake blocker

There is enough direction to continue company-level discovery without making the founder choose a product or model first. Founder/team fit and company-specific forecasts remain blocked on facts only the founder can supply: team capabilities/access, Foresee's relationship/current execution, and meaningful LOI terms. Until then, rank opportunities on market evidence and keep financials at Stage A.

## Red Flags
- Company-specific forecast is not credible until founders report team, product execution and commercial access facts.

## Yellow Flags
- European LOIs may unlock valuable discovery, but terms and transferability to U.S. venture-scale business remain unknown.
- User wants $1M ARR quickly; this goal must not become an ungrounded timeline.
