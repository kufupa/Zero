# Customer Voice — Insurance Comparison and Shopping

**Phase:** Startup-competitors Wave 2 / Positioning alternatives research
**Project:** insurance-shopping-agent
**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Confidence:** Medium that the cited individuals reported these experiences; Low for prevalence or representativeness.

---

## Method and limits

Exa Agent mined public reviews from Trustpilot, Better Business Bureau, Google Play, WalletHub and company-hosted testimonials; also searched Reddit and other forums. The output supplied **20 purposively selected excerpts**, not a random sample. Reddit did not yield usable direct dated firsthand evidence in the research run. Trustpilot snapshots and aggregate ratings have mixed vintages. User allegations were not independently verified; labels such as “Verified” or “Invited” do not prove all details. No frequency percentage or population prevalence can be asserted.

## Provisional pain themes

| Theme | Specific evidence | What it might mean | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Re-entering information after referral/redirect | Multiple direct service reviews mention another carrier/site requesting the same data | Measure fields/time repeated and compare completed quotes, not only starts | Medium that it occurs; Low prevalence |
| Quote estimate differs from verified/purchase price | Reviews and company terms acknowledge further verification can change rates | Show estimate vs. verified quote vs. bind; explain missing underwriting inputs | Medium that price changes occur; Low prevalence/causality |
| Control over calls/emails | Some EverQuote, Zebra and Jerry reviewers report repeated calls; attribution is disputed in at least one Jerry developer response | Test granular consent, recipients, clear opt-out/revocation | Low for systemic attribution; higher for consumer concern as a discovery topic |
| Agent handoff interrupts checkout | One Jerry Google Play review describes payment failure, agent rerouting, repeated information and higher premium | Instrument full quote→bind flow and preserve context through support | Low prevalence; specific failure deserves controlled test |
| Coverage comparison may be unclear | Selected users praise lower cost/more coverage; exact declarations/limits are absent | Coverage normalization must be verified, not marketed from anecdote | Low |
| Existing services do help | Positive excerpts praise simple setup, limited calls, good rates, agent assistance | Do not claim the category is broken; identify specific segment/job where alternatives fail | Medium as counterevidence |

## Interpretation for positioning

The best evidence supports a **job hypothesis**, not an incumbent-bashing story: “help me compare meaningful coverage choices and finish a policy purchase without repeating the work or losing context.” The verbatim review sample repeatedly uses “same info,” “double work,” “repetitive,” and calls/agent hand-off language. However, positive reviews and incumbent features contradict any blanket statement that comparison services fail. Test whether repeated entry/handoff is frequent enough and costly enough for an agency, partner, or consumer to pay for a better workflow.

## Research gaps

- No primary interviews or founder-specific customer evidence.
- No representative review sample or denominator for complaints.
- Sparse home-only quote-shopping evidence; coverage-specific data is missing.
- No matched carrier quote, policy document or bind confirmation to validate alleged price/coverage changes.
- No validated willingness to pay, preferred customer, or switching trigger.

## Red Flags

- Treating selected complaint snippets as proof that comparison platforms systematically fail would be unsupported.

## Yellow Flags

- Some dates are review/experience dates; relative Trustpilot dates remain relative.
- Call attribution, final premiums, identity and actual transactions are not independently established.
