# Pricing & Monetization Landscape — Insurance Shopping

*Skill: startup-competitors | Generated: 2026-09-30*

## Executive finding

“Free for consumer” is common because a different party pays. Free is a distribution/monetization structure, not differentiation. There are four distinct options below; do not double-count a commission plus a lead payment for one transaction without a contract and legal basis.

## Consumer price and payer map

| Model | Consumer price | Who pays / event | Publicly evidenced examples | What is private / uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Licensed agency commission | Generally no separate shopping fee; Jerry discloses that some policies/carriers/states may carry an initial fee | Insurer/agency commission when policy binds; possible renewal stream | Insurify, Jerry, Nsure, The Zebra, Policygenius | Commission %, payout timing, renewal rights, clawbacks, appointment terms, per-policy margin |
| Lead / referral marketplace | Free | Providers pay for qualified click, data lead, call, or match—may be payable on delivered inquiry rather than bound policy | EverQuote, LendingTree/QuoteWizard, MediaAlpha; NerdWallet/Bankrate | Rate card, lead acceptance, exclusivity, duplicate/rejection/refund, consent, realized bind attribution |
| Embedded/API/white-label | Usually no visible user fee | Contracted platform, usage, referral or revenue-share economics paid by partner | Insurify; Experian/Gabi; dealer/finance distribution | Rev share, API rate, minimums, integration cost, attach rate, partner acquisition cost |
| Agency SaaS/workflow | Not a consumer price | Agency pays subscription, users, usage and possibly implementation | Canopy Connect; EZLynx/PL Rating use quote-based pricing | New product willingness-to-pay, churn, service burden, integration and sales costs |
| Consumer subscription | No broad paid comparison subscription verified in reviewed examples | Consumer recurring payment for ongoing service | No named comparison broker’s public recurring paid plan established | WTP, retention, continuous value between renewal events |

## Published price evidence

- **Canopy Connect (agency intake/workflow):** annual-billing equivalent Scratch $100/month ($1,200 upfront annually); Elevate $250/month ($3,000/year); Peak $500/month ($6,000/year). Monthly-billing prices shown as $120/$300/$600. Includes 30/100/300 Quote Points and 3/5/10 users. Additional points $4/$3/$2; commercial intake costs two points and is excluded from Scratch. Confirm current tiers and contract details directly.
- **EZLynx / Vertafore PL Rating:** no verified official universal dollar rate card in sources reviewed; sales/custom pricing. Do not use third-party price estimates as fact.
- **Consumer marketplace:** generally free; neither commission percentages nor per-lead price sheets were public.

## Stage A formulas — no forecast yet

1. **Lead model:** revenue = acquired shoppers × completion × legally eligible/accepted share × billable inquiries per shopper × contracted net price. Contribution subtracts acquisition, data, servicing, contact/refund and compliance costs.
2. **Licensed broker:** revenue = eligible shoppers × quote completion × bind rate × contracted net commission + realized renewal income. Contribution subtracts CAC, licensed service labor, payment handling, reversals and ongoing service.
3. **Agency copilot:** revenue = paying accounts × ACV + contracted usage/implementation. Buyer ROI = measured labor saved + attributable incremental policy contribution − software/integration/support cost.
4. **Embedded:** revenue = incremental eligible issued policies × contracted share OR permissible platform/usage fee. Define attribution, consent, reversals, servicing and cancellation first.
5. **Consumer subscription:** revenue = active paying users × subscription price × retention; each input is unknown, and category norms are free.

Do not populate revenue with premium volume. Missing inputs include founder/customer, launch state, licenses, access, pricing, volume, bind conversion, CAC, retention and staffing.

## Pricing psychology / switching costs

- Consumer free is a standard acquisition price; monetization disclosures and result ranking matter to trust.
- Canopy uses usage points as value metric and charges overages; annual billing lowers monthly-equivalent price but commits cash upfront.
- Agency raters’ hidden/custom prices imply sales friction; no reliable public switching-cost score found. Integrations, carrier appointments, AMS migration, training and historical client data are likely dependencies, but must be validated with agencies.
- Consumer switching is comparatively low at shopping time but constrained by inertia, carrier discounts, avoiding coverage gaps and effort.

## Red Flags
- No public price benchmark tells us what a new consumer broker can earn or how much it costs to acquire/bind a policy.
- Lead and commission models depend on different operations and legal boundaries; do not treat them as freely interchangeable.

## Yellow Flags
- Company-reported revenue is not contribution; public-company operating metrics have different definitions and should not be compared without reconciliation.
