# Stage A Financial Gate — Inputs Before Projections

**Phase:** Startup-design Phase 7 groundwork (not a projection)  
**Project:** insurance-shopping-agent  
**Date:** 2026-09-30  
**Financial Model Stage:** **A — Assumption-Based, not yet numeric.** Interviews deferred; no founder-approved customer/model/assumptions.

## Why free can work

The consumer is often not the payer. A carrier may pay a licensed agency after policy bind; providers can buy accepted leads/clicks/calls; an embedded partner can share contracted distribution economics; an agency or platform can pay SaaS/API fees. These mechanisms are not interchangeable; product conduct, licensing, permitted compensation and written contracts constrain them.

Public validation of different mechanisms: EverQuote FY2025 $692.5M revenue, primarily qualified inquiries; LendingTree FY2025 Insurance segment $711.88M from matched inquiries/clicks/calls; Canopy publishes agency intake plans $100–$500/month equivalent with annual billing. These establish category-level commercial precedent, not startup price/CAC/retention.

## Scenario framework (inputs not supplied)

| Input | Consumer licensed agency | Lead marketplace | Agency SaaS / embedded |
|---|---|---|---|
| First paying party | Carrier/agency after eligible bound policy | Carrier/agency after accepted inquiry | Agency/platform/partner contract |
| Product event | Quote → verified bind → service/renewal | Consent → qualified delivery → acceptance | Workflow usage / integration / incremental outcome |
| Revenue formula | Binds × actual net commission + retained renewal commission | Accepted billable events × signed net event rate | Paying accounts × actual ACV + contracted usage |
| Acquisition cost | Cost per reached shopper / issued policy | Cost per accepted buyer-ready inquiry | Sales, onboarding and implementation per account |
| Variable costs | Licensed service, quote data, support, reversals, fees | Media/traffic, consent, call/data operations, credits/refunds | Integration, support, data access, hosting, onboarding |
| Core proof required | License/appointments + actual binding cohort | Written buyer contract + accepted-lead price/criteria | Signed pilot + measured ROI and renewal intent |
| Unknowns | All volume, commission, bind, retention, CAC inputs | All qualified lead rates, prices, repeat, CAC, consent | Buyer count, ACV, sales cycle, churn, implementation margin |

No numerical projections are included: founder has not selected a model or supplied reachable volume, pricing/payer hypothesis, team size, timeline, budget/runway or distribution access. Filling these in would violate Stage A honesty rules, not improve them.

## Minimal assumptions checkpoint

Before calculating Month 1–12 and Year 1–3 cases, founder must choose:
1. First product/buyer (consumer, carrier/agent lead, agency SaaS, embedded platform).
2. One initial state/line and product boundary (estimate, quote, advise, submit, bind, service).
3. Payer/event/price assumption (or actual written partner offer).
4. Reachable customer volume/channel, team/contractor load, launch date, operating budget and funding runway.
5. Which functions are licensed/in-house vs. provided by partner and actual compliance/service costs.

After those are provided, build conservative/base/optimistic scenarios, ±30% sensitivity, cash/runway and break-even formulas; mark each number **[Assumption—unvalidated]** until measured.

## Red Flags
- Do not map premium volume or incumbent revenue directly to startup revenue.
- Licensing and compensation review must precede an executable financial model.

## Yellow Flags
- No customer interviews or user behavior data; deferred by request.
- Public prices are mostly absent for lead/referral and agency rater systems.
