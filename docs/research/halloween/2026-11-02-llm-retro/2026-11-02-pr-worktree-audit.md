## External research and reusable loop

**Claim:** More concurrent agents alone do not guarantee faster delivery. A [2026 controlled benchmark preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10263) compared dynamic concurrency on/off over 354 tasks and 2,124 executions: mean runtime increased in 14/15 agent–benchmark combinations, and token use was 1.41–3.31× sequential. The same paper found up to 14.3 percentage points better task pass rates on its longest-horizon benchmark. These are benchmark outcomes for specific agent configurations, not a production forecast.

**Strongest counterexample:** [Co-Coder](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00953v1) reports dependency-aware parallel scheduling improved held-out pass rate from 56.8% to 68.1% and generation latency by 1.81× on DevEval. Its latency stops at the final agent and **excludes testing**; it does not establish faster accepted deployment here. Parallelize truly independent work and measure the integrated result; do not infer that all parallelism is bad or that any claimed multiplier transfers to Zero.

**Context and skills:** A [2026 study of repository context files](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988) found longer context did not generally improve task success and increased inference cost by over 20% on average. This studied context files, not our specific skills. Keep reusable instructions focused on recurring mistakes and test their effect rather than assuming more memory produces speed. [METR's 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) says selection bias and concurrent-agent timing make its new real-world productivity estimates unreliable. Do not present an old slowdown estimate as the current effect.

**Proposed local experiment, not a proven multiplier:** Count time from starting a comparable task to an accepted integrated behavior, including PR and check queue, human review time, rework/escaped defects and abandoned attempts. Compare a small single-agent baseline with dependency-aware parallel work on similar tasks, same gates and current model/settings. Add writers only where interfaces, shared files and environment ownership are clear and integration can accept their output. No universal WIP number follows from the available evidence.

**Bounded loop:** For each slice, record one user-visible acceptance example; inspect current main plus relevant open PRs/branches and dirty worktrees; assign the actual missing delta and owner; run focused risk-appropriate checks through the project's approved route; record SHA, commands/results/skips, next step and unresolved holds; integrate and verify the combined behavior. Stop on success, repeated identical failure, missing oracle or out-of-scope edits rather than accumulating branches. Do not auto-authorize deployment or real-guest writes. This matters because the [Looping Is Not Reliability preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24604) observed correctness falling from 82.0% after one forced revision to 67.3% after two in 900 three-revision trajectories across 30 HumanEval repairs. That is a small artificial benchmark, not a measured probability for this product. The earlier [loop-engineering source note](loop-engineering-research.md) distinguishes Boris Cherny's workflow anecdotes from controlled evidence.
