## Verdict

**The run built substantial foundations, but did not establish the connected staff workflow the early milestones promised.** The earliest intended fake booking was still unproved roughly 4 hours 26 minutes after its 20:54 target. The plan assumed that the Cloud create bridge was already on the starting branch; it was not, and the safety binding between staff approval and dispatch was also incomplete. Those were discovered four minutes after kickoff. Several other milestones then slipped, even while eight lanes visibly produced work.

The complete pilot's original forecast was 01:32, approximately 5 hours 50 minutes after go. The owner paused at 01:20–01:25, **before that final time**, so it is inaccurate to say the final deadline had already passed. By then, however, the planned first booking, several intermediate milestones, integration and proof were still incomplete; the plan's remaining work could not responsibly be represented as nearly finished. The later ~06:00 and 15-minute estimates were explicitly withdrawn.

**Three more hours are a useful target, not a justified full-pilot ETA.** I would aim first for one selected-email → staff Go → fake Labtest save → fresh readback, while continuing genuinely independent work in all six lanes. That could expose and remove the central integration blockage, but even this first proof is conditional on its missing seams and checks. A full six-workflow deployed pilot in that window is possible only if several unproved dependencies resolve quickly; current evidence does not support a completion probability or promise. There is credible business opportunity, but no measured staff-time or financial ROI yet.

## Is there evidence of real business value?

There is a credible opportunity, but **no measured staff time saved or demonstrated financial ROI yet**. In a 30-day Centurion corpus, a retrospective model-labelled analysis estimated that **727 of 1,204 actionable inbound emails** could receive at least partial help from five relevant task categories *if the assumed capabilities existed*. It uses model labels and sometimes later replies; it is not a current accuracy, adoption or automation rate. Earlier **74%** estimates included additional capabilities outside this pilot, so they should not be presented as this pilot’s coverage ([corpus audit:32](/home/ubuntu/projects/zero/docs/research/halloween/b2b-dashboard/2026-11-02-centurion-corpus-audit.md:32)).

To demonstrate value, compare a small, representative set of incoming requests and independent staff responses with the same cases handled by Nava, record whether each outcome is correct and safe, and time staff effort including review, failures and corrections. For example, a 10–20-email stratified exploratory sample can identify obvious problems; it cannot establish production accuracy. Do not give Nava a later staff reply as part of the incoming request. Measure actual usage and rework once approved staff use is possible. Real-mail evaluation is optional under the current product bar; this is a proposed ROI measurement, not an invented release gate.

A usable calculation is `staff hours saved = unique eligible cases × adoption × (average manual minutes − average assisted minutes) / 60`, where assisted minutes include review and rework **across all attempts**, including failed or escalated cases. `Net value = hours saved × loaded staff cost per hour − operating cost`; financial ROI is `net value / operating cost`. Do not multiply a successes-only saving by all cases or subtract rework twice. No required input is presently measured well enough to quote a dollar return.
