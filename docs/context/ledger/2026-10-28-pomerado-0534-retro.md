## What helped and what hurt

1. Private successful-observation capture resolved the earlier blindness: it showed twelve completed reads without the requested target, rather than treating execution counts as progress. Its redaction, completeness and truncation checks do not by themselves prove visible-screen coverage.
2. Failed executions now supply specific script-context and navigation error signatures. The new mint gets a narrow hint about browser-side DOM evaluation and navigating to a fresh page context. Its effectiveness is not established.
3. The correctable Guardian refusal needs its actual reason respected, not a bypass. Its rationale referred to an unrelated URL; the retained evidence does not identify what ran or whether any external navigation happened.
4. The key collision stopped the next guard before browser work. The retry waited for a free observation and then reacquired both keys. A free count is not a lock, and we did not stop or inspect another hotel's process.
5. The first exact-label classifier failed on an unexpected nested object and was corrected. A later process-environment classifier could count the root cgroup twice; its matching environment values were valid but its raw counts are not independent process counts. Neither observer defect explains the mint failure.
6. Prior success ETAs were superseded and no new success ETA has a measured basis. Public-only Exa Agent reported $0.5001. Azure spend and per-call provider model/effort receipts remain unknown.
