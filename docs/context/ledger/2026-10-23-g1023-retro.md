## 2. Da Vinci pilot failure (the most important finding)

**What Faith saw:** she opened mail, and Nava read the rooms (she liked that). The agency was extracted but never pre-matched. Rates failed with a generic "HMS could not complete the lookup. Retry", and a toast said "Staff quotation installation unavailable".

| Fact | Evidence |

| The refusal is `RESERVATIONIST_STAFF_QUOTE_CONFIGURATION_HRDAV` unset on all 4 procs; no Da Vinci descriptor exists on EC2 | `staff-quote-installation.ts:154-168`, `staff-quote-run.ts:616-622`, `/proc` 12:22Z |

| The banner hides the reason; the pane never says "Da Vinci quotes are not set up yet" | `nava-pane.tsx:252` → `STAFF_QUOTE_FAILED` generic |
| Known since 2026-10-22: the D1 Greptile P1 "runStaffQuote installs HRCEN" was deferred, and the DV retro said "DV quote blocked: Centurion-only rate-policy source" | SR3 retro §2, DV install retro |
| Plan step A5 said "set MAKE_QUOTE=on … turn it off if the score shows a mismatch". The score had n=0, so the condition could never fire | state.md:157-159 |

**Root cause (process):** a flag was treated as the feature. "Switch on" was taken to mean "works", but the dependent configuration was never checked, and no live canary ran on the exact property. Exa research agrees: "evaluating a flag before the SDK receives its state … returns a fallback value". A feature is on only after its config contract and an end-to-end canary pass ([LaunchDarkly](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/getting-started/setting-up), [GitLab flag-state E2E](https://docs.gitlab.com/development/testing_guide/end_to_end/feature_flag_testing/)).

**Fix list (L2 §4):**
1. Da Vinci descriptor: tenant, owner `{cdpUrl 127.0.0.1:19225, borrowed, HRDAV, secretPath}`, outputDir, ratePolicy.
2. **Da Vinci rate policy needs real Da Vinci documents** (contract/agency invoices, rate+board codes). Without them, only public rates can be priced.
3. Policy `ownerRef` is singular per transport, but Da Vinci has 4 transports. This needs per-transport selection or multi-owner support.
4. Board codes for BLRB/BARDIS/DEF are unknown, and Da Vinci has no dinner default.
5. Property-targeted party/availability prefetch for HRDAV.
6. Startup readiness check: `MAKE_QUOTE=on` ⇒ the HRDAV descriptor must parse with `owner.propertyCode=HRDAV`, else fail readiness.
7. Pane copy: "Da Vinci quotes aren't set up yet" instead of the generic "Retry".

## 9. Model routing (Exa evidence + our numbers)

- **Supported:** "Once a frontier planner has collapsed the ambiguity into a detailed, explicit instruction, less expensive models simply have to follow it". Every model mix produced similar quality, while costs ranged 8× ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics)). Cognition: dynamic frontier+sidekick at "60% lower cost" ([Devin Fusion](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-fusion)).
- **Against:** the cheapest tier is not for complex agentic coding ([Anthropic Haiku 5.5](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5)). Sidekick setups need tuning, and retries can erase the savings. No study isolates "cheap writer + strong reviewer" for our kind of work.
- **Our data:** lanes are 34% of Grok spend and the orchestrator is 61%. **The lever is the orchestrator, not the coders.** Moving lanes from Grok 4.6 to Luna saves at most ~$186/night. Fixing orchestrator context saves ~$207/night with no quality risk.
- **Proposal (test 1 night, A/B):** orchestrator = a stateless tick that rebuilds from state.md, with a hard 150K handoff; coders = GPT-6 Luna max for small explicit units (≤300 LOC, spec + tests named), Grok 4.6 for write paths/HMS tooling; reviewer = Grok 4.7 xhigh + Greptile; HMS proofs = deterministic scripts in their own lane. Measure accepted PRs, review rounds, escaped P1s, and $ per PR.
